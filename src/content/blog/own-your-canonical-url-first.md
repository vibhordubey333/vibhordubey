Publishing on your own site first changes the emotional shape of writing.

The draft no longer starts by asking, "What will the algorithm do with this?" It starts by asking, "What do I want to keep?"

## A personal site is not just a destination

It is the place where the most complete version should live:

- the original headline
- the original formatting
- the original metadata
- the canonical URL that search engines and readers can return to

Syndication still matters. Platforms are where people already spend time. But there is a difference between **distribution** and **ownership**.

## The workflow I want

1. Write the post in Markdown.
2. Publish it to the personal site.
3. Let the site deploy and establish the canonical URL.
4. Adapt the piece for Medium, dev.to, or anywhere else.
5. Add those outward links back into the original post.

That last step matters more than it first appears. Once the source post links back out to every syndicated version, the archive becomes self-documenting.

## The calm benefit

Your site stops feeling like a copy of your writing life. It becomes the center of it.

And once that is true, syndication becomes a distribution strategy instead of an identity strategy.
